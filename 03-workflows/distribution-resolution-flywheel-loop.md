# Distribution → Resolution → Flywheel Operating Loop

## Purpose

Unify the three transcript-derived operating models into one governed ANAS OS loop.

- **Distribution** increases useful surface area: attention, trust, qualified demand, opportunities, feedback, and talent flow.
- **Resolution** converts uncertainty into evidence-backed decisions about market, product, team, technology, economics, distribution, and operations.
- **Flywheel** turns trusted delivery and customer value into cash, bounded reinvestment, reusable capability, and better outputs that create new distribution.

The loop is an ANAS OS subsystem capability. It never outranks Constitution, governance, policy, authority, gates, security, legal constraints, or explicit human approval requirements.

```text
             DISTRIBUTION
       attention • trust • demand

             RESOLUTION

                 ↺
```

## Operating protocol

### 1. Start with the constraint

State the current bottleneck or opportunity. Do not start by choosing a feature, hire, channel, or technology.

Record:
- objective
- current evidence
- assumptions
- decision horizon
- risk and reversibility
- owner

### 2. Read the distribution surface

Capture useful signals from content, products, customer work, partnerships, referrals, communities, and other legitimate touchpoints.

Do not optimize for vanity metrics alone. Prefer qualified reach, repeat exposure, useful engagement, inbound quality, conversion signals, customer outcomes, and compounding opportunity flow.

### 3. Increase resolution before expensive commitment

Classify each major department or problem area as **sure-shot** or **R&D**.

For R&D:
- write a falsifiable hypothesis
- timebox the trial
- cap cost and blast radius
- define success evidence and exit rules
- prefer vertical slices and fast feedback
- kill or redesign weak paths quickly

For known work:
- model capacity
- choose delivery path from existing staff, hiring, technology, contractor, outsourcing, or training
- include recruiting lead time and operational overhead

### 4. Deliver something that creates proof

Build the smallest end-to-end slice that can produce evidence and customer value. Do not mistake a detailed plan for a validated shape.

### 5. Feed the flywheel

Measure:
- trust signals
- talent attraction and retention
- customer value
- economics and cash generation
- experimentation throughput

Treat trust, distribution, and similar composite scores as proxies. They are decision aids, not direct measurements of reputation, revenue, or causality.

### 6. Reinvest only within governance

Positive operating surplus may justify bounded R&D, capability building, better tooling, or capacity increases. Reinvestment is never automatic and must respect ANAS OS authority, financial controls, and risk boundaries.

### 7. Convert learning into reusable capability

Capture validated methods as:
- playbooks
- skills
- templates
- tooling
- training material
- partner/mentor networks
- hiring criteria
- project patterns

Adopt changes through the normal evidence → proposal → review → approval → adoption path.

### 8. Re-enter distribution

New work, outcomes, lessons, tools, and proofs can create useful distribution. Route qualified feedback and opportunities back into the next resolution cycle.

## Decision rules

- Do not scale a low-resolution graph merely because more funding or people are available.
- Do not use distribution numbers as a substitute for customer value.
- Do not use revenue as proof that every underlying method is correct.
- Do not turn a temporary R&D workaround into permanent architecture without evidence.
- Do not treat AI as an automatic replacement for human judgment; select it when it improves a bounded workflow.
- Keep experiments reversible wherever practical.
- High-risk, irreversible, external, financial, legal, security-sensitive, or customer-data actions remain subject to the ANAS OS approval model.
