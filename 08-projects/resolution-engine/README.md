# Resolution Project Engine

The Resolution Project Engine digitalizes the Video 2 project model: uncertain projects begin with a low-resolution map, then gain resolution through action, experiments, feedback, and progressively denser organizational design.

## Core loop

`Frame → map departments → minimum viable team → classify sure-shot/R&D → vertical-slice alpha → measure → learn → increase resolution → capacity math → choose delivery path → redraw when needed → beta → gold/release → playbook`

## Objects

- **Project Resolution:** confidence across market, product, team, technology, economics, distribution, and operations.
- **Department:** a problem/output area, not a job title.
- **Role:** a concrete responsibility introduced to solve a department problem.
- **Trial:** bounded R&D with a hypothesis, timebox, cost ceiling, and evidence target.
- **Vertical Slice:** a small end-to-end slice used to test the complete system.
- **Alpha:** experimental phase where redraw is allowed.
- **Beta:** shape is mostly known; improve quality and validate conversion.
- **Gold:** release candidate meeting quality, operational, security, and release gates.
- **Capacity Model:** output rate × duration × buffer for staffing and technology decisions.
- **Resolution Graph:** validated department/role/technology/vendor/mentor structure.
- **Playbook:** reusable resolution graph plus validated know-how and constraints.

## Rules

1. Start with a rough map; never pretend early certainty.
2. Action must produce information; experiments have evidence targets.
3. Define departments before roles and roles before hiring depth.
4. Build breadth before depth with a minimum viable team.
5. Mark each department `sure-shot` when its method is understood and scalable; mark it `R&D` when the method is uncertain.
6. Concentrate R&D on one or two deliberate innovation fronts.
7. Use vertical slices to test integrated outputs rather than isolated components.
8. Do not scale the organization graph until evidence shows the shape is stable enough.
9. Select existing staff, permanent hiring, contractors, outsourcing, technology, or training based on method, capacity, economics, urgency, and reversibility.
10. Keep buffers explicit; numeric defaults are assumptions, not universal laws.
11. Distribution feedback is a resolution input: prototypes, pilots, playtests, public artifacts, and customer feedback can improve the map and attract talent/mentors.
12. Funding should follow resolution rather than hide uncertainty.

The objective is not to predict the entire project perfectly. It is to increase the quality of the map fast enough that expensive commitments become justified.
